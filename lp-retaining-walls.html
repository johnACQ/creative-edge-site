Why walls fail

The part that decides whether a wall lasts is the part you never see.

Blocks are the easy bit. What holds a wall up is the base under it and the drainage behind it, and that is exactly where a rushed job cuts corners.

! The wall starts leaning or bulging a few seasons in.
! Water sits behind it with nowhere to go, and finds your foundation instead.
! You end up with a wall, but the slope above it is still unusable.
! You send the enquiry and never get a call back.
We build from the base up and we drain it properly. Then we design what sits on top, so a wall turns a slope you walk past into space you actually use.

Can you give me a rough number before I commit to anything?

It's a budget, not a final quote. Site access, drainage, rock and material choices can move it, and we'll tell you which ones apply to your yard while we're standing in it.

Why do retaining walls fail?

Almost always the base or the drainage. If the base isn't deep enough or properly compacted the wall settles unevenly, and if water can't get out from behind it the pressure pushes the wall forward. Both are invisible once the job is finished, which is why they're the first things cut on a cheap quote and the reason we start there.

Do I need a permit or an engineer for my wall?

It depends on the height, what's above and below it and what your municipality requires. We'll tell you straight at the consult whether your project needs that step, and if it does we'll work with whoever needs to be involved rather than leaving you to sort it out.
