Landscape lighting: plan it in, don't staple it on

After dark, the yard you paid for disappears. The stone, the trees, the water feature: once the sun drops behind the hill, you can't see any of it, so you stop using the space. Lighting gives you those evenings back: a warm Okanagan night on the patio, the wall and the water lit, and an easy walk back to the door. It works when it goes in at the right time.

Why lighting ends up stapled to a fence

Lighting is the item most often left to the end. By then your planting is in, the patio is down and the walls are up, so the only place left to run cable is along the top of a fence or across the lawn. You end up with lights where the cable could reach, not where they should be.

Planned in, it is the opposite. The cable goes in while your beds are open, before the planting and the patio close everything up.

Light your yard in three passes

Plan it on the drawing in three passes. Each one does a different job.

Pass one: features. The trees, stone and water you already paid for. A lit wall or water feature becomes the thing you look at from the kitchen window at night, all year round.

Pass two: paths and steps. Anywhere your feet go after dark has to be safe to walk: the path to the fire, the steps between levels, the way back to the door. Low path lights along the bed edge do that job without lighting up the whole street.

Pass three: where you sit. The patio, the kitchen, the fire. Light it for using the space on a warm night, not for a floodlit parking lot.

Do the three passes on paper and every cable run gets planned with the yard. Skip them and you are guessing with a staple gun.
